A personalised mRNA vaccine cut melanoma recurrence by 42% in a phase 3 trial
In 1,137 patients whose high-risk melanoma had been removed, adding Moderna and Merck’s intismeran to Keytruda cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% versus Keytruda alone, results presented at the ESMO congress in Madrid show.
WHY  Each vaccine is built from the mutations in a patient’s own tumour, so the immune system learns to spot those cells if they return; the ICR’s Alan Melcher calls it strong proof of principle but wants longer survival data. Next: a US filing, and trials in kidney, bladder and lung cancer.

The White House says Melania Trump brokered a local ceasefire so aid can enter the Russian-held town on the Dnipro and civilians can leave by a designated route. Russia’s general staff said its forces would hold fire on the Kherson axis.

Today’s question
What would you build if you trusted your own taste over the consensus?
ESSAY  sashachapin.substack.com · Sasha Chapin
You can judge a book by its cover ↗
Chapin argues that if you listen without judging, people reveal most of who they are within minutes, through posture, eye contact and the way they tell their own stories. His example: someone retelling a work dispute can show a victim mindset without saying so, and over years this perception quietly sorts which relationships deepen. The surprising turn is that people who see your blind spots often stay silent on purpose, out of compassion, until you’re ready. It pushes your note on wanting friends to feel safe confiding in you.
ESSAY  benkuhn.net · Ben Kuhn
Be less scared of overconfidence ↗
Kuhn, an engineer now at Anthropic, argues that safe rules like “experts are usually right” make you throw away specific knowledge you actually have. His own case: he valued startup equity by deferring to VC prices, ignoring what he knew about Wave’s growth and founders, and badly underrated it. The hook is the asymmetry: underconfidence is usually the costlier error, but its damage is invisible, because no one sees the ideas that were never tried. It speaks to your note on thinking for yourself instead of outsourcing it.
ESSAY  karpathy.bearblog.dev · Andrej Karpathy
Animals vs Ghosts ↗
Karpathy argues that frontier labs are not building animal-like minds that learn from experience; they are summoning “ghosts,” intelligences shaped by human text. Pretraining does the job evolution does for animals, a crude but dense starting point, which is why a zebra can run minutes after birth and a model can write with no lived experience at all. The open question: can ghosts be pushed toward learning from their own experience, or will they stay as different from us as planes are from birds?

WORKFLOW  addyo.substack.com
Agentic verification ↗
Osmani argues the bottleneck in agent-assisted coding has moved from writing code to knowing it is correct, so the highest-leverage move is giving the agent checks it can run itself. Concretely: write the end-to-end test for your one key user flow before prompting, add one property test (“the cart total always equals the sum of its items”) that a library hammers with thousands of inputs, and keep the test command fast and quiet. The catch he leaves open: if the same agent writes the feature and the test, both can share one misunderstanding.
