Two Italian technicians killed in drone strike on Kuwait; Iran denies role
Two Rheinmetall Italia employees maintaining an air-defense surveillance station in Kuwait died Thursday after drones hit the site; a Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were wounded, Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani said. Hawaiian lava may carry carbon that leaked out of Earth’s core
A Columbia-led team measured carbon isotopes in microscopic droplets of magma sealed inside crystals from Kīlauea. Typical mantle carbon reads between −5 and −7 per mil; Kīlauea’s read near −1, a value rarely seen. The study is in PNAS.
WHY  Kīlauea is fed by a plume rising from near the core–mantle boundary, and the heavy carbon matches carbonaceous meteorites from the outer solar system: some of the carbon in living things may have arrived from space, sunk into the core, and come back up through volcanoes. Lead author Yves Moussallam says more measurements are needed.

Boehly’s Eldridge leads US-backed deal for Lukoil’s foreign assets
A consortium of Eldridge, the US International Development Finance Corporation, UAE-based Catena and Xtellus Partners agreed Wednesday to buy Lukoil International: oil fields, refineries, the Litasco trading arm and retail stations across several continents. The price was not disclosed; the deal needs Treasury (OFAC) approval.
WHY  Sanctions forced the sale, and an earlier Carlyle agreement failed to clear regulators. Payment is in restricted Russian shares held for five years or until sanctions lift, so no cash reaches Russia, and the government lender takes equity at no upfront cost; Democrat Gregory Meeks says it sidesteps a congressional ban on the agency investing in Russia.

FCC clears SpaceX to launch up to a million AI data-center satellites
The FCC gave conditional approval Wednesday to SpaceX’s Starmind constellation at altitudes of 500 to 1,002 km, over objections from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society. SpaceX must fly its first satellite by November 2033 and half the fleet by 2038.
WHY  SpaceX argues near-constant solar power makes orbit the cheapest place to run AI compute; opponents raised debris, interference and feasibility concerns. The FCC has not yet approved its debris plan for satellites above 600 km, so this is permission to try, not a launch schedule.

POEM  anthology.lib.virginia.edu · John Keats
Ode to a Nightingale ↗
In the spring of 1819, months after his brother died of tuberculosis, a 23-year-old Keats hears a nightingale and wants to dissolve into its song and leave behind a world where youth grows pale and dies. The poem has lasted because it states a paradox exactly: the joy is so complete it aches, and the bird seems immortal only because the listener is not. Eight stanzas, about five minutes; watch for the single word that turns the whole poem, and read it twice.
ESSAY  bsubercaseaux.github.io · Bernardo Subercaseaux
Mathematics, philosophy, not-poems, and learning to surf from a book ↗
A Chilean theoretical computer scientist argues that learning math from a book is as silly as learning to surf from one: math is a verb, and books are companions while you do it. His working moves are concrete: first understand why anyone loved the game (a teacher’s story about Hipparchus turned him from a failing 14-year-old into a mathematician), name the specific obstacle blocking a proof and recurse on it, test cases with code before writing anything elegant, and keep working after the proof is done. He walks through the messy real route to a clean result about numbers written only in 0s and 1s, the route textbooks hide. A direct answer to your note on how “I’m not good at math” becomes a reason to quit.

GUIDE  medium.com · Tony Zhou & Taylor Ramos
Every Frame a Painting’s postmortem: the working habits behind great video essays ↗
The pair behind one of YouTube’s best video-essay channels published the script of the episode they never made, listing the habits that built their style. Keep a dated notebook and let weak ideas be forgotten; turn each premise into one question and test it casually on friends; write every point on flash cards and rearrange until the argument has “steel in the walls”; tag every clip with keywords so surprising cuts surface. They budget about 8 hours of editing per finished minute, which is why the argument gets fixed before the timeline. First move: rewrite one short note from your site as a single question and ask it offhand to one friend today; if nobody bites, it needs more time.
