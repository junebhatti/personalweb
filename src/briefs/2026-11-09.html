A hidden calf gives right whales their best birthing season since 2009
Researchers photographed an unrecorded calf off Quebec’s Anticosti Island with its 38-year-old mother, Silt, raising the season to 24 calves, AP reports.
WHY  New England Aquarium scientists think the pair slipped away before winter surveys recorded them together. It matters because about 380 whales remain, threatened by ship strikes and fishing gear. Next: a fight over delayed US protections.

Newborn deaths in Flint halved after cash payments to mothers, study finds
In JAMA Pediatrics, neonatal mortality fell from 10.2 to 5.1 per 1,000 births a year after Rx Kids began, while the rest of Michigan moved from 4.0 to 3.9.
WHY  The program pays $1,500 in pregnancy and $500 a month in infancy; the authors propose that economic security reduces preterm birth and low birth weight. Next: whether the drop holds over more years and in newer Rx Kids communities.

Who would you still make things for if no one new ever saw them?
ESSAY  Substack · Du’Bois A’Keen
Thank God I’ve Never Gone Viral: Fame, Identity & Timing ↗
A’Keen, who has toured since age twelve, argues that two decades of obscurity built the self-knowledge that visibility would have tested too early. A stranger in a coffee shop remembering an old Apollo performance sets off his case that obscurity was “the workshop,” not the waiting room. He admits he still wants big rooms, so the essay never fully settles. It complicates your note on being content with the small cohort who know you.
ESSAY  Anamita’s Newsletter · Anamita Guha
What We Hand Over: What Happens to Human Agency in the Age of AI Agents ↗
Guha splits agent use into delegation, where you know what you gave away and why, and abdication, where no decision ever happened. She uses Instinct and Meta’s Muse daily and builds such products, so the worry comes from inside. The hard part: from outside, the two look identical. Her test is two questions: what did you hand over on purpose this week, and what without deciding? It pushes your note on agents trading autonomy for free time.
CLASSIC  Last Essays of Elia · Charles Lamb
The Superannuated Man ↗
After decades at a London counting-house desk, Lamb’s narrator is unexpectedly pensioned and handed all his time at once. He drifts through Bond Street on working mornings, revisits the office to find his hat peg taken, and jokes about naming a son Nothing-To-Do. It has lasted because it treats free time as a shock, not a prize. It connects to your note on losing the days you used to plan.

WORKFLOW  Latent Space · Mihail Eric
How Stanford Teaches AI-Native Engineering ↗
Eric, who teaches Stanford’s CS146S, argues that agents now finish most classic assignments in one prompt, so what’s left to train is judgment. His ten-week course starts by having students write a minimal coding agent, about 200 lines, then moves through context, skills and agentic review. One habit for this week’s hackathon: before merging an agent’s change, make sure you can explain the diff.

AI RESEARCH  Dwarkesh Patel
The benchmark where AIs have to lie to win ↗
Patel ran 386 games of Blood on the Clocktower, teams of one model against another, to measure bluffing and deduction. GPT-6 Astra beat Claude Opus 4.6 about 92% of the time, mostly through better play on the good side. In one game Claude players took a missed shot at face value while GPT players treated it as inconclusive and later caught the sabotage.
