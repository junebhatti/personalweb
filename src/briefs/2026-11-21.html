Early humans skipped Arabia’s ancient lakes because there was no stone

Essays & Arguments
Two arguments about how much of you is borrowed. Twain says nearly every opinion you hold came from the people whose approval you need. Two psychologists answer that a century of famous studies made the self look emptier than it is, and that the findings which survived replication point to stable people who shape their situations. Read together, the question is less whether you are influenced than which parts of you hold when the room changes. Craig Mod gives a builder’s version: make tools to your own shape instead of renting someone else’s defaults.

CLASSIC  paulgraham.com · Mark Twain
Corn-pone Opinions (1901) ↗
Twain’s claim is that a coldly reasoned, first-hand opinion almost never exists: we take our views on fashion, morals, politics and religion from the people around us, because self-approval comes mainly from their approval. He starts from an enslaved young man named Jerry, who preached from a woodpile that you can predict a man’s opinions from where he gets his bread, then argues Jerry did not go far enough, since most conformity is unconscious, not calculated. It has lasted because it reads like a field guide to group chats and markets, and because Twain includes himself, admitting he studied the tariff question to the bottom and came out empty. About 1,600 words; it sharpens your note on identity versus environment.
ESSAY  Psyche · Jack W Klein & William B Swann
How psychology’s classic studies misled us about the self ↗
Two social psychologists (Swann developed self-verification theory) argue that the “empty self” idea, where a warm coffee or washed hands steers your judgments, has collapsed: in one replication effort only 17% of social-priming findings held up. They reread the classics too: Milgram participants who gave shocks scored higher on diligence and helpfulness, and the Stanford prison study likely drew authoritarian volunteers. Personality and situation turn out to have effects of similar size. Set it beside Twain and decide who wins.

Software Bonkers ↗
A writer and photographer in Japan, “OK-but-not-great” at code, describes building software for an audience of one: a Twitter where posts vanish after seven days and you can post twice a day, and, in five days, TaxBot2000, a local Python, Flask and SQLite app that handles his multi-country, multi-currency finances better than a decade of Quicken. DESIGN  joshwcomeau.com
Designing Beautiful Shadows in CSS ↗
Fuzzy grey box-shadows are one of the quickest tells of a generic, generated UI; Comeau shows how to make depth look real. Pick one light source and keep every shadow’s offset ratio the same (vertical twice horizontal); as an element rises, increase offset and blur and lower opacity; stack three to five thin shadows instead of one; and tint shadows with the background’s hue instead of black, which desaturates. First move: open his Shadow Palette Generator from the guide, copy the small/medium/large tokens into your site’s CSS, and swap them onto one card (under 10 minutes).
