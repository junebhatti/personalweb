Israel votes in its first general election since the October 7 attack
Israelis vote today for the 120-seat Knesset in a race AP describes as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, whose Likud faces a bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot.
WHY  Netanyahu presents himself as a survivor and coalition builder with religious and nationalist partners; the opposition says he must answer for the October 7 security failures and his refusal of a state inquiry. With 61 seats needed and no party ever reaching that alone, the next step is coalition talks that could run for weeks, with Mansour Abbas’s Ra’am a possible kingmaker.

FDA approves a targeted drug pairing for prostate cancer with DNA-repair mutations
The FDA on Monday approved Pfizer’s talazoparib with enzalutamide for metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer with HRR gene mutations. In the 599-patient TALAPRO-3 trial, the combination cut the risk of radiographic progression or death by 52% versus enzalutamide alone (hazard ratio 0.48).
WHY  Talazoparib is a PARP inhibitor: tumors that already cannot repair DNA through the HRR pathway lose their backup repair route and die, while hormone therapy attacks the cancer’s growth signal. Overall survival data are still immature, so the next readout is whether the delay in progression becomes longer lives.

4 
A painted-on liquid stopped tooth decay without drilling in a large child trial
A Phase III trial of 830 children under six, published in JAMA Pediatrics, found that silver diamine fluoride applied every six months stopped decay in more than half of affected baby teeth, with no drilling, injections or anesthesia.
WHY  Silver kills cavity-causing bacteria and fluoride hardens the remaining mineral, which matters because cavities are the most common chronic disease in children and the hardest cases often end in surgery under general anesthesia. The trial supports an FDA application to approve the liquid specifically for arresting decay, which would move it off-label.

ESSAY  Terence Tao’s blog · Jeremy Avigad
The Future of Mathematics ↗
A logician and philosopher argues that AI can now produce results that once counted as respectable papers by recombining known techniques, so mathematicians must move toward harder problems, bigger self-chosen questions and new ways of using the tools. His mechanism: reinforcement learning rewards only final outcomes, which can yield cleverness without strategy, and no reward signal could have picked Riemann’s world-changing lecture topic. The open question he leaves is less whether AI can make Grothendieck-scale leaps than what humans value in doing math at all. It extends your note on building as an act of agency.

The bullish and bearish signals in Anthropic’s IPO filing ↗
Anthropic filed its prospectus Monday, and the numbers show both concentration and depth: about 2,000 customers pay $1 million or more a year and 250 pay $10 million or more, yet the five largest made up about 25% of revenue in the first nine months. Nearly half of first-half revenue flowed through Amazon, Google and Microsoft cloud marketplaces, companies that are also its investors, suppliers and competitors. The open question is the almost $650 billion in computing commitments it must pay whether or not demand shows up.

NBA signs CFTC pact, names Kalshi and Polymarket preferred exchanges ↗
The NBA became the third league, after the NHL and MLB, to sign an information-sharing agreement with the CFTC, and named Kalshi and Polymarket its preferred prediction-market exchanges. It lands while states call the contracts unlicensed gambling and the NFL lobbies for state oversight. Leagues are choosing to legitimize the markets and police insiders from within rather than fight them.
