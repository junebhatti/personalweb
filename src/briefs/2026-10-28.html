A hidden cause of severe bowel disease, found in the blood
An Oxford-led study of more than 4,900 people with inflammatory bowel disease found that about 3.5% carry antibodies that neutralize IL-10, a protein that normally holds gut inflammation in check.
WHY  It explains why a gene variant linked to severe IBD 30 years ago matters, and suggests a blood test could identify these patients. Next: treatments aimed at the antibodies, or the cells that make them.

Essays & Arguments
Two pieces today make the same case from different rooms. Kristen Freeland opens a half-renovated house to her family and finds that the mess is what let people in. Robin Sloan spends a week building an app with four users, all of them his family, and calls it cooking. Neither cares about scale. Both suggest the useful question is not how to remove friction from life, but which friction is holding something up, and what small, slightly inefficient thing you could make for the people already near you.
 
Today’s question
Which inconvenience in your life would you keep on purpose, because it keeps someone close?
ESSAY  Substack · Kristen Freeland
Interruptable ↗
Freeland argues that loneliness may depend less on how many people we know than on how willing we are to be inconvenienced by them. Her daughter’s fourth birthday, held mid-renovation with store-bought food, put cousins who mostly knew each other through FaceTime at one table, and became a monthly “(not)Perfect Potluck” with paper plates and no cleaning. She admits she loves convenience and is not giving it up, so the real question is which interruptions to protect. Feeds your note on convenience eroding connection.
ESSAY  robinsloan.com · Robin Sloan
An app can be a home-cooked meal ↗
Sloan argues that software made for a handful of people, with no plan to scale, is closer to home cooking than to a product. He built BoopSnoop, a family-only messaging app, in about a week (half of it lost to code signing); it has four daily users and they still use it years later. With agents, a week of work shrinks toward an afternoon, which leaves the harder question of what you would build if your only users were people you love. Feeds your note on building when ideas feel scarce.

A home-cooked app for six friends
Build a tiny shared board for a monthly low-effort dinner, where anyone can claim a date and say what they’ll bring, so gathering gets one tap easier without getting any less real.
1In Claude Code’s Plan Mode, describe one page: upcoming dates, who is coming, what each person brings. Ask for a single HTML file backed by PocketBase.
2Run PocketBase locally, have Claude wire the page to it, and write a CLAUDE.md with your conventions so the next session starts where you left off.
3Put it online, text the link to six friends, and claim the first date yourself.

AI MODELS  BleepingComputer
OpenAI ships GPT-6.1 Terra for cheap computer use ↗
OpenAI’s new mid-tier model is built for agents that click through software. It costs $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output, half its predecessors, runs more than three times faster, and scored 50.3% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus 21.5% for GPT-5.6 Terra. When computer use gets this cheap, the bottleneck in automating your own chores moves from cost to knowing what to automate.
