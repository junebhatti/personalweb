US readies new tariffs on 16 trading partners over “excess capacity”
Three people familiar with the plan told Reuters the administration will imminently release findings of a Section 301 probe into China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, India and ten others, with new duties stacked on existing ones. Rates are expected to vary by country and to respect caps in signed deals, such as 15% for the EU, Japan and South Korea. Nasdaq futures fell more than 2% on the report.
WHY  The administration says subsidized overproduction undercuts US manufacturers and is rebuilding the tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February; China says its exports reflect competitive advantage. Watch the USTR release and whether the US–China truce, which caps tariffs on Chinese goods and expires January 10, is extended.

Far-side Moon rocks say the early Solar System’s bombardment faded slowly
Argon dating of impact-melt fragments returned by China’s Chang’e-6 records collisions spread from 4.33 to 1.13 billion years ago, with no single spike, according to a Science Advances study announced by Curtin University this week.
WHY  The far side was reworked less by later geology than the Apollo sites, so it keeps a cleaner record; a gradual decline undercuts the decades-old idea of a sudden “late heavy bombardment” around 3.9 billion years ago, the era when life on Earth was getting started.

Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for $41 billion in cash
Sanofi will pay $175 a share, about a 46% premium to Insmed’s unaffected November 18 close, for two marketed lung drugs, Brinsupri and Arikayce, plus a Phase III inhaled therapy; it expects to close in the first half of 2027.
WHY  Sanofi is buying revenue that already exists to reduce reliance on Dupixent; it is biopharma’s largest deal in years and a sign that buyers will pay full premiums for de-risked commercial assets.

Anthropic posts a Claude-written proof of a 47-year-old complexity conjecture
A 70-page paper credited to Claude claims VP ≠ VNP over every field, Leslie Valiant’s 1979 conjecture that the permanent needs superpolynomial-size algebraic circuits, with a Lean 4 formalization that passes machine checks. Anthropic’s README says no human mathematician has checked it.
WHY  Lean verifies every step but not whether the short file defining VP and VNP says what the conjecture says, so the claim now waits on humans reading that file; it is the algebraic cousin of P vs NP and, by the paper’s own account, does not settle it.

Essays & Arguments
Today’s two pieces are about the parts of us that act before we decide. In Tintern Abbey, a remembered valley keeps arriving uninvited in city rooms and does its work anyway. In the Jumping Frenchmen of Maine, a startle turns grown men into mirrors of whoever shouted. One is the involuntary at its most generous, the other at its most humiliating. Both suggest the self you plan sits on top of one that simply responds, and the useful question is which reflexes can be trained and which can only be noticed.
 
Today’s question
What comes back to you without being asked, and what has it been doing for you?
POEM  UVA Anthology · William Wordsworth
Lines Written a Few Miles above Tintern Abbey ↗
Wordsworth returns to a river valley after five years away and takes stock of what the place did for him while he was gone. The mechanism is memory as a reserve: in lonely rooms and loud cities the remembered landscape kept returning on its own, steadying him and, he suspects, shaping small kindnesses he cannot trace. About 160 lines of blank verse and a seven-minute read, it lasted because it made the case that feeling recalled in calm is where meaning gets made, and late on it turns somewhere you will not expect. It sits right beside your note on involuntary memory.
ESSAY  The Public Domain Review · Ross Bullen
“Men with Queer Nerves”: The Jumping Frenchmen of Maine ↗
In the 1870s, French-Canadian lumberjacks around Moosehead Lake would leap, strike or echo any command, even Homer recited in Greek, the instant they were startled. The neurologist George Beard tested them in person and concluded the cause was mental, not physical: a lifelong, extreme version of how little inhibition anyone has between a cue and an act. His work fed Gilles de la Tourette’s, and similar syndromes turn up in Malaysia, Japan and the Philippines. The open question Bullen leaves is how much was nerves and how much was the isolation and contempt the men lived under.

DESIGN  Butterick’s Practical Typography
Typography in ten minutes ↗
Matthew Butterick’s claim: get the body text right and most of a page’s quality follows. Four numbers do it: 15–25px type on the web, line height at 120–145% of the font size, 45–90 characters per line, and one deliberately chosen font instead of a system default. Those are exactly the knobs that make vibe-coded sites look generated: edge-to-edge paragraphs, default line height, Arial. First move: in your site’s global CSS, set body text to line-height 1.35 and max-width 65ch, then compare a notes page before and after.

PREDICTION MARKETS  Advisor Perspectives · Bloomberg
Kalshi wants to be the inflation exchange ↗
Kalshi is pitching banks and hedge funds on CPI and core-CPI futures with fixed expiries, designed by ex-Barclays trader Michael Ashton and targeted for December. Dated contracts answer CME chief Terry Duffy’s complaint that institutions cannot hedge with perpetuals, and aim at a $2.2 trillion TIPS and $5.4 trillion inflation-swap market with no exchange-traded future. CME’s 2004 attempt did not stick; whether this one does decides if prediction markets graduate into ordinary derivatives.

Three scenarios for OpenAI’s listing as it weighs a delay to hold out for a $1 trillion valuation.
