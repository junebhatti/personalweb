Japan launches the first mission to bring back soil from a Martian moon
JAXA’s MMX spacecraft lifted off on an H3 rocket from Tanegashima. It is due to land on Phobos and return samples to Australia in 2031, the first ever from a moon other than our own.
WHY  Nobody knows whether Phobos is a captured asteroid or debris from an ancient impact on Mars; rock in hand could settle it. Next: arrival at Mars in 2027 and a Phobos landing no earlier than December 2028.

Amazon deforestation falls to its lowest level since records began
Brazil’s PRODES satellite data show 3,335 sq km of Amazon cleared between August 2025 and July 2026, down nearly 42% in a year and the lowest since 1988.
WHY  The government credits tighter satellite monitoring and stiffer penalties for illegal logging. Next: the Oct. 25 presidential runoff; Lula targets zero illegal deforestation by 2030, and challenger Flávio Bolsonaro has pledged the same by 2029.

Today’s question
If every forecast is a recipe rather than a gauge, what does it mean to trust a number you cannot audit?
ESSAY  Silver Bulletin · Nate Silver
My pet peeve about the polling error debate ↗
Silver argues that polls are not a broken gauge that always reads the same amount too blue, so you cannot just correct for 2016 and 2020. Pollsters keep changing the recipe (weighting to past vote, likely-voter screens, herding), so even the direction of the next miss is uncertain. The part that matters most is correlation: treat Senate races as independent coin flips and Democrats look like 91% favourites; model shared error and it drops to the mid-70s.
POST  One Useful Thing · Ethan Mollick
30 Months on the Exponential (in Charts) ↗
Mollick lays out the curve in numbers: the length of task an AI can finish half the time went from about four minutes in 2024 to 17 hours by April 2026, and an exam answer that cost 30 cents now costs 0.04 cents. Progress stays jagged: superhuman at math, still weak at long-form writing. His open problem is control: delegating to agents is closer to “conjuring” than working, and you lose sight of what was actually done.
ESSAY  henrikkarlsson.xyz · Henrik Karlsson
How is music possible ↗
Karlsson asks how patterns of tone and rhythm can make trees look new through a car window or make strangers in a church cry together. He rejects evolutionary accounts as too thin (the Kalash hear minor chords as happy) and Schopenhauer’s metaphysics as explaining everything and so nothing. His answer is emergence: music needs its own higher-level concepts, as a dune cannot be read from one grain. The open question is whether that gap can ever be explained or only marvelled at.

WORKFLOW  geoffreylitt.com · Geoffrey Litt
Understanding is the new bottleneck ↗
Litt argues that checking an agent’s output is not enough. To keep steering a project you need a working mental model of it, or you build up “cognitive debt.” His fixes come from teaching: an /explain-diff skill that writes a prose “literate diff” ordered by logic rather than by file, a five-question quiz you must pass before shipping, and “micro-worlds”, small tools an agent builds so you can do the work yourself and learn it.
Try this week~3 hrs
Make your agent teach you
Turn Litt’s explainer-and-quiz loop into a reusable skill for one of your own vibe-coded projects.
1Write an /explain-diff skill: background first, the goal before any code, prose ordered by logic, not by file.
2Have it end every explainer with five questions, and don’t merge until you can answer them unaided.
3Run it on your last three changes and note which answers you missed. That is where your model of the code is thin.
