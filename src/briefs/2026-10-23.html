Essays & Arguments
Three writers on how much of a life should be steered. Sasha Chapin lost the felt sense of choosing and became more effective. Visakan Veerasamy sees his grand projects failing for lack of a small loop beneath them. Ben Kuhn says agency and taste separate equally skilled people. Together they sharpen your note on surrender: dropping a rigid plan is not dropping direction.
 
Today’s question
Can you let go of a plan without letting go of your direction?
ESSAY  Sasha’s Newsletter · Sasha Chapin
Biting the no-self bullet ↗
Chapin argues that “no-self” literally describes what happened to him: two years ago the background tension he called his self dissolved, and he still functions fine. The strange part: he no longer feels he chooses anything, yet acts with more agency than before. It extends your note that you are not your thoughts: maybe you are not the chooser either.
ESSAY  visakanv · Visakan Veerasamy
surf’s up! ↗
Visakan concludes he keeps losing by starting projects too grand for his time; the fix is smaller work where winning means enjoying the sentences. His model is SimCity: as a kid he spent his budget on roads around the map’s edge and got fired, never having learned the core loop of zone, power, settle, tax, reinvest. It reframes your building note: the idea bottleneck may be a loop problem, not an inspiration problem.
ESSAY  benkuhn.net · Ben Kuhn
Impact, agency, and taste ↗
Kuhn, who left the startup Wave for Anthropic, argues that among equally skilled people the difference is finding high-leverage work, which takes agency (initiative plus persistence) and taste (a feel for what will work). His tactics: work backwards from the root goal, announce intentions instead of asking permission, treat ambitious projects as bets. Good taste, he says, feels less like being smart than like everyone else being oddly bad at something. A counterweight to your note on surrender over rigid plans.

WORKFLOW  harper.blog
How to find magical travel experiences using an AI travel buddy ↗
Harper Reed lets a model interview him for 30 minutes, one easy question at a time, then has it compress the answers into a taste profile of 250 words or fewer. He adds standing instructions (search in the local language, prefer first-party sources, check whether places are open) and puts it all in a project so every chat inherits it. Vibe comparisons beat category searches. The reusable lesson is the pattern: interview, compress, reuse.

PREDICTION MARKETS  CBS News
Pentagon bars troops from betting on military operations with inside information ↗
Pete Hegseth signed a memo on Oct. 20 barring personnel from using nonpublic information to bet on US military operations, naming Polymarket and Kalshi; his reasoning is that adversaries watch these markets, so a trade is itself a signal. The tension: the inside knowledge that makes a market accurate is what makes it dangerous.
AI AGENTS  simonwillison.net
Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation ↗
Anthropic is handing its Agent Skills standard, the instruction folders agents load on demand, to the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation, where it already put MCP. Skills look set to become shared plumbing rather than one vendor’s feature; the open question is who else adopts them.
