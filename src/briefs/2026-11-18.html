Eritrea becomes the 34th country to eliminate trachoma
The WHO validated Eritrea as having eliminated trachoma, the world’s leading infectious cause of blindness, as a public health problem: the 11th country in its African Region to do so.
WHY  Two decades of the WHO’s SAFE approach (eyelid surgery, mass antibiotics, face washing, clean water and sanitation) reached remote rural districts with limited outside funding. It matters because 92 million people still lived in trachoma-endemic areas as of March 2026.

FDA approves the first drug for a rare autoimmune kidney disease
The FDA approved Gazyva (obinutuzumab) as the first drug specifically for adults with primary membranous nephropathy, in which the immune system attacks the kidney’s filters and protein leaks into the urine.
WHY  In the 142-patient MAJESTY trial, 36% on Gazyva reached complete remission at two years versus 6% on tacrolimus, a drug used off-label until now. Patients who can progress to kidney failure now have an approved option aimed at the disease itself.

Essays & Arguments
Both of today’s pieces are about seeing yourself only after you’ve been pushed out of the action. In Crane’s story, a wounded lieutenant walks back from the line and finally sees the battle he was inside: generals, batteries, stragglers who know more than the fighters. In Kirwin’s essay, a flood of machine prose makes her hear her own voice as a stranger would, so she goes digging for the dialect words no model would treasure. In both, the injury is what forces the look.
 
Today’s question
Can you understand a pain while you’re still inside it, or only once it has sent you to the rear?
ESSAY  Don’t Be Trash · Angela Kirwin
And honestly? I started to worry I’d morphed into a chatbot ↗
A Mancunian writer living in Italy suspects her sentences now sound machine-made, and that dodging AI tics only makes her sound less human. Her evidence is dialect: Manchester slang for rubbish comes from the leftover bobbins of the cotton mills, and the delight of tracing that history is exactly what a model can copy but never feel. Her fix is half joke, half manifesto: never call her writing content again, and keep the regional words and misspellings as human fingerprints. It pushes your note that work which can’t be scored stays yours.
STORY  SUNY Jefferson · Stephen Crane
An Episode of War (1899) ↗
A lieutenant is dividing his company’s coffee into neat squares with his sword when a bullet from an unwatched wood hits his arm, and his own sword suddenly becomes a strange object he cannot sheathe. Walking to the rear, he finds the wound has changed everyone around him (men shy from touching him, a passing officer scolds him for bandaging it wrong) and changed what he can see, as the battle he was fighting turns into something like a painting. Crane’s cool, ironic eye is why eight minutes of it still reads as modern; the story turns on a decision at a field-hospital door. It speaks to your note that pain is wasted without the space to reflect on it.

WORKFLOW  Prime Radiant · Simon Willison
External sandboxes for coding agents ↗
The agent stays on your laptop, but every command it runs goes to a separate Linux sandbox that sees only the folders and websites you allow. You can try it with two npx commands; Claude Code joins by routing its Bash commands through one environment variable, and an allow-list proxy can attach API keys so the agent never sees them. The point is safety that doesn’t depend on a model judging each action, which matters most when you start experimenting with cheaper or local models.

PREDICTION MARKETS  CoinDesk
Kalshi halts Trump mention markets on CFTC order ↗
The CFTC stayed Kalshi’s contracts on what the president will say and opened a proceeding into whether Kalshi falsely certified them, citing its September advisory that such markets hinge on one person’s conduct and may be presumptively open to manipulation. Kalshi says it is complying, while a Vance mention market stays live; the open question for the industry is whether any contract a single insider can move becomes off-limits.
AI AGENTS  CoinDesk
Google finds an AI agent that writes and fires exploits on its own ↗
Google’s threat team says it found, on an exposed attacker server, the first tool it has seen fully automate finding and exploiting software flaws with no human in the loop. Named exploit_agent, it watched popular web software for security patches, diffed the code before and after each fix to locate the hole, then had commercial and local LLMs write and run the exploit: the same loop you use to fix bugs, pointed the other way.
