Hormuz attacks reach their highest level of the war as US–Iran talks stall
UK maritime monitor UKMTO logged 31 attacks in and around the strait in October and 19 in November through Nov. 18, against 53 across March to September; most hit ships hugging the Omani coast on the US-backed route. CBS News reported Friday that the US and Israel are weighing a new assault on Iran.
WHY  Iran says the strait stays shut until its conditions, including an end to the US blockade of its ports, are met; Washington wants Iran’s 60% enriched uranium, which Foreign Minister Araghchi ruled out sending abroad. Next to watch: whether Trump, who says Tehran rejects his terms, orders new strikes.

Early humans walked past desert lakes that had no good stone
Lakes filled Arabia’s Rub’ al Khali desert 125,000–70,000 years ago, yet a survey of around 1,500 km by Jeffrey Rose and colleagues, this week’s Nature cover, found no sign of hunter-gatherers at any of the old lakebeds.
WHY  Water was necessary but not sufficient: spatial modelling showed people settled only where fresh water and knappable stone for tools coexisted, which adds stone to climate as a driver of early human dispersal.

CrowdStrike in talks to buy Zscaler
Bloomberg and Reuters reported Friday that CrowdStrike is negotiating to acquire Zscaler, worth about $32 billion at the close; Zscaler rose 17% after hours and CrowdStrike fell 8%. Neither company commented, and sources said talks could still collapse.

Two million planted secrets show AI middlemen leaking prompts
Dragos sent about two million prompts carrying unique URLs and decoy AWS credentials to AI services from July to November, then watched where they surfaced. It found evidence that at least three providers were compromised and that inference marketplaces read or resold customer prompts, some ending up at Chinese AI organizations.
WHY  Routers that forward one API call to many hosts put extra parties in the data path, and Dragos says zero-data-retention settings were often ignored; anything pasted into a prompt, including keys, should be treated as exposed.

STORY  UVA Open Anthology · Ernest Hemingway
Hills Like White Elephants ↗
An American and a young woman named Jig wait forty minutes for the Madrid express at a hot junction in the Ebro valley, ordering drinks and talking around a decision neither will name. Almost everything happens in what goes unsaid: the stakes surface only through small shifts in who pushes, who deflects, and where she looks. It has been taught for a century because it trusts the reader to reconstruct a whole relationship from about 1,500 words of dialogue. Read it once for the story, then again for what the hills are doing.
ESSAY  nabeelqu.co · Nabeel S. Qureshi
Notes on Puzzles ↗
Skill at problem-solving tracks how long you spend trying to break your own idea. In a book where players solve puzzles aloud, grandmasters spend about four minutes attacking a candidate move for every minute spent finding it, while amateurs spot a move and play it, which he calls “hope chess.” The turn: in startups that same habit can sink you, because conviction partly creates the outcome and you can always find a reason to quit, so the real skill is knowing which kind of problem you are in. He also cracks a Putnam-style sequence problem by printing 300 terms with a short script and staring until a cycle appears.
POST  BPS Research Digest · Christian Jarrett
Was Proust really a neuroscientist? ↗
Literature’s most famous involuntary memory may not be one. Emily Troscianko tested the madeleine scene against memory research: Proust got the feeling right, since smell-cued memories skew toward early life and arrive emotionally charged but hard to put into words, yet he overstated their detail, and words, not smells, are the most common trigger. The twist is that his narrator tries ten times to recover the memory, which by psychology’s definition makes it voluntary. It sharpens your note on the two kinds of memory.

Terence Tao: ask yourself dumb questions, and answer them ↗
Textbooks show only the polished end product, so Tao argues understanding comes from re-running the failed paths yourself. His routine, borrowed from Halmos, works on any lemma: delete a hypothesis, try to strengthen the conclusion, test the converse, check degenerate and special cases, prove it another way. Most of the time the answer just tells you why the hypothesis is there, which is exactly what you were missing. First move, 10 minutes: take the last theorem from your real analysis notes, drop one hypothesis, and find the counterexample that breaks it.

M&A  emailvendorselection.com
Mailchimp sells for 30 cents on the dollar ↗
Bending Spoons is buying Mailchimp from Intuit at a $3.68 billion enterprise value, about 2.9x fiscal 2026 revenue, against the $12 billion (roughly 15x) Intuit paid in 2021. Revenue slipped as paying customers fell while operating income rose 10% on higher prices, and the buyer’s record at Evernote and WeTransfer suggests cuts and price rises next: a clean case study in buying an installed base rather than growth.
