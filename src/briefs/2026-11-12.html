At COP31, governments sign on to double the world's solar homes by 2030
A Global Rooftop Solar Declaration unveiled at the UN climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, commits signatories to 300 million solar homes by 2030, up from roughly 150 million today. Early signers are mostly Global South governments, including several small island states.
WHY  The Global Solar Council argues rooftop panels plus home batteries shield households from this year's fossil-fuel price swings. The target is voluntary; signers pledge six steps, from easier permits to lending against rooftop systems, while storage and grid upgrades remain the gap.

Global fossil-fuel CO2 emissions are projected to fall in 2026
The Global Carbon Budget, from 90-plus institutions led by the University of Exeter, projects fossil CO2 down 0.4% to 38.2 billion tonnes, with China, the US, the EU and Japan all falling. Total emissions including land use still hit a record 43.3 billion tonnes, driven by Southeast Asian peat fires.
WHY  The authors credit solar, wind and EVs, which now meet about two-thirds of new energy demand, plus a temporary oil shock from the Iran conflict. Co-author Glen Peters cautions that one down year is not a peak unless structural change drives it.

CLASSIC  Quotidiana · Robert Louis Stevenson
On the Enjoyment of Unpleasant Places (1874) ↗
Stevenson, writing about a treeless, windswept northern coast he dislikes, argues that enjoying a place is a skill you train, an “austere regimen in scenery.” His method: look from several angles, borrow eyes from books and old prints, and when the big view fails, shrink to grasses, crackling whin-pods, and the warmth behind a wall in a gale. It has lasted because it treats attention as something you do, not something that happens to you. About 10 minutes. Feeds your note on taste mattering more than technical skill: taste as trained seeing.
ESSAY  Substack · Consciously Lost
The Trouble With Being Seen ↗
A writer with about 500 subscribers admits to checking the dashboard and watching it quietly change why they write, from enjoying the work to wanting to be read. The mechanism is the counting itself: a silent subscriber and a reader who writes back register as the same number, and every like creates an urge to like back. It is short and candid, and it leaves open whether getting paid makes the pull worse. Pushes on your note about being content with the small cohort who know you.

WORKFLOW  Anthropic docs
Prompting Claude Fable 5.2: say who owns the code ↗
One sentence changes how the model treats your project: “You are the owner and long-term maintainer of this codebase” raised how often it consolidated duplicated code from under 10% of test runs to about 75%. The cost is 70–80% more output and unrequested edits, so leave it out when you want a small, contained change; a “no refactors” rule does not cancel it. The guide also shows a completion line that roughly halved sessions ending with unfinished work. Worth testing on your next solo project: same task, with and without the ownership cue, then read both diffs.
