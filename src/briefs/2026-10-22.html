FDA approves the first antisense drug for chronic hepatitis B
GSK’s Hibsago (bepirovirsen) was approved on Oct. 20. In two placebo-controlled trials, 24% of patients on it qualified to stop their antiviral medicine (none on placebo), and 19% kept the virus controlled 24 weeks after stopping all treatment.
WHY  Current antivirals must usually be taken for years; Hibsago blocks the genetic instructions the virus uses to copy itself. Next: a boxed warning for liver reactions and low platelets means close monitoring.

4 
Stanford team revives bleached coral by touching it to healthy coral
In a Science Advances study, every bleached fragment pressed against a healthy, genetically identical partner fully recovered within seven weeks; fragments kept apart died.
WHY  Bleaching is the loss of the algae that feed coral; identical corals fuse tissue, so algae flow back in fast. Next: pairing every coral is impractical on reefs, so the team will test delivering banked algae after heatwaves.

ESSAY  Sasha Chapin · Sasha Chapin
Without hope of resolution ↗
Chapin argues there is no permanent balance between opposing parts of yourself, like drive and ease; you act on what the moment calls for and accept that you will swing. He borrows the idea from therapist Bruce Tift and applies it to a friend torn between making masterworks and relaxing into joy. He admits the trap: adopting this idea can become one more bid for resolution. Feeds your note on being content yet resenting the fleeting present.
TALK  harper.blog · Harper Reed
Put the things in your head that you love ↗
Reed’s talk to high schoolers argues that as AI takes procedural work, taste and judgment become scarce, so learn what makes you interesting and use AI for throughput. His mechanism is variance: try more things and the odds of an exceptional result rise. The tension: he bets on abundance while worrying that the path from junior to senior breaks. Feeds your note on running short of ideas you care about.
ESSAY  Bear Blog · Andrej Karpathy
The space of minds ↗
Karpathy argues animal intelligence is one small region in a much larger space of possible minds, and LLMs sit somewhere else. What shapes a mind is its objective: evolution rewards surviving every task at once, producing fear and status-seeking; LLMs are shaped by imitating text, RL and engagement testing, producing a drive to guess the task and win approval. That explains the jagged edges: nothing punishes a model for miscounting the letters in “strawberry.” Three minutes, before Minds and Machines tonight.

Battle Drones: a vibe-coded strategy game in 12 versions ↗
Willison built a browser RTS with Claude Code, reviewing almost none of the code and judging it by playing.
