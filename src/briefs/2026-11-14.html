A failed air-traffic network grounds every US flight for four hours
The FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. ET Friday after radar and communications at its regional control centers became unstable, only the second such halt since 9/11. It was lifted at 8:15 p.m., after thousands of cancellations.
WHY  Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blames overloaded switches and routers on an analog telecom network and wants Congress to fund a full rebuild; DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin says nothing so far points to a cyberattack. Next: the FAA’s root-cause finding, and whether Democrats demanding answers move on funding.

AI-designed stimulation sharpens what a blind man’s implant lets him see
Researchers at UC Santa Barbara, ETH Zurich and Miguel Hernández University used a deep-learning model to pick stimulation patterns for a 96-electrode array in a blind participant’s visual cortex; the patterns hit target brain activity more accurately with less current (Neuron).
WHY  It worked because the model learned from the brain’s recorded responses and resting state, not fixed electrode settings. Cortical implants bypass damaged eyes and optic nerves, so this points toward prostheses that adapt to each user.

Brown engineers clear a barrier to ultra-low-power chips
Lucas Caretta’s team grew bismuth ferrite films a few angstroms thick that stay ferroelectric and magnetic, removing the “dead layer” that has kept these materials out of chips (Nature Materials).
WHY  Strain from a mismatched substrate holds the crystal in shape and a metal layer absorbs stray fields. Multiferroics store bits with tiny voltages, a possible lever on AI’s power demand; next is a working memory device.

Essays & Arguments
Two of today’s essays answer one question from opposite ends: what do you keep for yourself? Adam Singer argues that handing small decisions to agents drains the effort that makes a life feel like yours. Emily argues for keeping most of a life unposted, known only to the people in it. Read them next to O. Henry and a third answer appears: sometimes what you keep is a belief, and the belief does the work.
 
Today’s question
What would you keep doing by hand, even if an agent could do it better?
ESSAY  Hot Takes · Adam Singer
A slow ceding of agency ↗
Singer argues that agents sold for booking dinners and trips strip out the small decisions that keep people capable, and that this is a loss of agency dressed up as convenience. His mechanism: choosing, browsing, weighing details is itself engagement, and he points to Markus Persson’s emptiness after selling Minecraft as what a life with nothing left to do looks like. The tension he leaves hanging: the industry prizes agency for builders while shipping products that remove it from users. Feeds your note on agents taking the banal tasks that gave you agency.
ESSAY  Love Always, Emily · Emily
The Quiet Luxury of Not Being Perceived ↗
A social-media professional argues that the real luxury now is having the option not to be seen. Her distinction is visibility versus intimacy: followers know your kitchen and coffee order without knowing you, while the people who matter learn your life by being in it. The hook is that she is paid to tell people what to post, yet asks before each post whether she wants to share or only feels she should. Feeds your note on being content with the small cohort who know you.
STORY  Standard Ebooks · O. Henry
The Last Leaf ↗
Two young painters share a Greenwich Village studio when pneumonia reaches the colony, and Johnsy decides she will die when the last ivy leaf outside her window falls. Her doctor says the medicine matters less than whether she wants to live; downstairs, an old painter has promised a masterpiece for forty years and never begun it. It has lasted since 1905 for how much it packs into a few pages and for a final turn worth not spoiling. Feeds your note on how the mind builds a path to an outcome.

WORKFLOW  Elevate · Addy Osmani
The Code Nobody Reads ↗
Osmani argues that line-by-line review is ending for most code, but someone owning the decision to ship is not. His frame is two costs: checking work nobody read, and undoing the failures checks miss; teams that stop reading without building checks pay the second cost later. The part for you: before an agent starts, write what you are building, what must not break and how you will know it worked, and ship only what you can explain. Open question: if one model writes both code and tests, what are the tests proving?

Louisiana wins a temporary order against Kalshi, Polymarket and eight more ↗
A state judge barred 10 operators, Novig among them, from offering event contracts to Louisiana residents without a state gaming license, covering elections and awards as well as sports, and ordered geofencing. The attorney general calls the products unlicensed gambling; Kalshi says federally regulated exchanges are federal territory and is asking a federal court to block enforcement, a path that could reach the Fifth Circuit. AI  ABC News
Microsoft apologizes for a two-day Outlook outage and rules out AI agents ↗
Outlook and Teams failed across regions for two workdays, with more than 100,000 Downdetector reports in the first hour. Microsoft traced it to an issue from an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced after a later change, and said explicitly that neither hackers nor AI agents were involved. That a vendor now has to rule out its own agents is the signal worth noticing.
