Iran says an LNG tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz
Iran’s Revolutionary Guards navy said a tanker carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and exploded on Friday while using an “unauthorized and dangerous route,” a day after Iranian media said four oil tankers were hit. No vessel details or independent confirmation were immediately available.
WHY  Iran says ships must use its approved lanes and heed its warnings; attacks have continued even as traffic picked up, with at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November after 37 in October, per the Washington Institute. Next: whether traffic falls again after Trump said Thursday the U.S. does not need a deal with Iran.

WHO: TB deaths fell again, and an overdue milestone was met
WHO’s Global Tuberculosis Report 2026 estimates 1.13 million TB deaths in 2025, down 4% from 2024, and says the first global milestone for cutting TB deaths has now been reached, five years late.
WHY  Faster diagnosis and shorter treatment: WHO-recommended rapid tests were the first test in 59% of diagnoses, up from 48% in 2023, and six-month regimens reached 42% of people treated for drug-resistant TB, up from 3%. Next: donor funding fell 14% to $0.94 billion in 2025, and poorer countries are struggling to fill the gap.

CERN physicists find first evidence of a one-in-ten-billion decay
LHCb reported the first evidence, at 3.2 sigma, of a beauty particle decaying into two muons, a process predicted roughly once in every ten billion such particles, plus the most precise measurement yet of a sister decay.
WHY  The upgraded detector records collisions at up to five times its old rate, and machine learning helped isolate the signal. Rare decays can reveal particles too heavy to make directly; so far the result matches the Standard Model, and 5 sigma is the next bar.

Essays & Arguments
Two of today’s pieces make the same trade a century apart. In Relative Obscurity a writer talks himself out of chasing an audience; in Maupassant’s Happiness a woman makes that choice so completely her old world forgets her. Together they suggest being known by a few is not a consolation prize. It is a different bet, with real costs.
 
Today’s question
Is being known deeply by a few people a smaller life, or a larger one?
ESSAY  The Art of Wandering · Michael Remedios
Relative Obscurity ↗
A financial planner admits he spent years equating being known with success, then argues for “relative obscurity”: writing for the people who actually read, not for the algorithm. The turn comes from two small scenes: a podcast listener calling him “the Art of Wandering guy,” which somehow left him feeling like nobody, and a friend texting to ask if he’d read their journal, which counted for more than thousands of views. He never quite resolves wanting to be seen while distrusting what does the seeing. It feeds your note on being known deeply by a few.
STORY  Standard Ebooks · Guy de Maupassant
Happiness ↗
Guests at a Riviera villa argue over whether love can last for years, until Corsica appears on the horizon and an old man recalls a night at an isolated farmhouse in its mountains. There he slowly realized the elderly woman had once belonged to his own polished world, and had left it entirely. About 1,800 words; it lasts because Maupassant won’t say whether her life was wasted or the fullest one in the story. It feeds your note on choosing closeness over recognition.
ESSAY  Bear Blog · Andrej Karpathy
Verifiability ↗
Karpathy argues the best predictor of what AI will automate is not whether a task can be specified but whether it can be verified. Neural nets learn anything whose attempts are resettable, cheap to retry and automatically scorable, which is why math and code race ahead while taste and strategy lag, the “jagged” frontier. Open question: does unscorable work ever catch up? It sharpens your note on agents taking over your day: what can’t be scored may be what stays yours.

WORKFLOW  Anthropic Engineering
How Claude Code’s auto mode decides what to run ↗
Auto mode lets Claude Code approve its own routine actions, because people were clicking yes on 93% of permission prompts anyway. Edits inside your repo run freely; shell commands, web fetches and external actions go to a two-stage classifier that sees only your messages and the agent’s tool calls, so the agent can’t argue its way past it. The hook is Anthropic’s own “honest number”: it still missed 17% of real overeager actions. Takeaway: give specific instructions, list the services you trust, and still read anything irreversible.
