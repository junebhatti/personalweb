First new drug in a decade for advanced head and neck cancer
The FDA granted accelerated approval to Rybrevant Faspro for adults whose recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma progressed after platinum chemotherapy and immunotherapy. In a 102-patient trial, 42% saw tumors shrink or disappear.
WHY  Approved options for these patients have response rates of about 20% or less, per the FDA, and none had been added in 10 years. The approval rests on response rates, so confirmatory trials must now show longer-term benefit.

England’s biggest infrastructure projects must now leave nature better off
Since Nov. 2, applications for nationally significant infrastructure projects (major roads, energy and water schemes) must deliver at least a 10% gain in biodiversity, measured with a statutory metric.
WHY  Gains must be maintained for at least 30 years, and the guidance ties them to wider recovery such as habitat corridors and natural flood management. The first applications filed under the rule are the test.

Today’s question
When you rehearse a future in your head, are you predicting it or choosing it?
STORY  UVA Open Anthology · Kate Chopin
The Story of an Hour ↗
First published in Vogue in 1894 as “The Dream of an Hour.” A woman with a weak heart is told, gently, that her husband has died in a railroad accident; she goes to her room alone, sits by an open window, and begins to imagine the years ahead. In about 1,000 words it maps a whole interior life, and it has lasted because it shows how fast a mind builds a future once it is allowed to. Five minutes; it feeds your note on the mental path to an outcome.
ESSAY  Substack · Lisa Strom
Taste was supposed to be the future, but it’s not ↗
Strom argues that the popular claim that taste will be the scarce human skill gets it backwards: taste is social and shaped by feeds, so its median form is already standardized and easy for machines to reproduce. Her mechanism: platforms reward the recognizable, and “profilicity” (an identity built for how others see you) makes people converge. The hook: if taste comes from culture, the scarcer skill is questioning the context that formed it. It pushes against your note that taste now beats skill.

WORKFLOW  Anthropic Engineering
The components of agent memory ↗
Agents have to decide what to remember before they know what will matter, so Anthropic proposes four layers (raw transcripts, notes, structured wikis and a small always-loaded index) plus an offline “dreaming” pass that checks notes against the logs and fixes them. In Pokémon Red, one model wrote 413 memory files and earned one badge; a later one kept about ten and earned four. For a personal agent: keep the index short, store only what it cannot see for itself, and schedule a review pass.

PREDICTION MARKETS  Silver Bulletin · Nate Silver
Models led prediction markets in forecasting the blue wave ↗
Silver argues markets followed the forecasting models in the midterms, not the reverse: prices converged on his FLIPR model (correlation of .99 or higher) and the model reached the right answer days to weeks earlier. Markets overreacted to election-day turnout chatter, though they won in Mississippi. The open question is whether markets need public models to be accurate at all; he discloses that he advises Polymarket.
