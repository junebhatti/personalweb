Houthis advance to within 60 km of Aden as Yemen’s government pulls back
Military and government sources told AFP the Houthis seized the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts of Lahj province and reached a camp 60 km from Aden, the interim seat of the internationally recognised government. The government said it completed withdrawing its forces from areas near the Bab el-Mandeb strait to defend the city.
WHY  Yemen’s dormant civil war reignited in September as a spillover of the US–Iran war; the Iran-aligned Houthis seek control of the Bab el-Mandeb coast, while the Saudi-backed government says securing Aden and nearby provinces is now its priority. Next: whether the Houthis press toward the city itself.

Species down to 50 animals can recover when people intervene
Researchers at the Zoological Society of London and Monash University reviewed 141 bird and mammal species that fell below 50 wild individuals since 1950: 61% of those given direct conservation recovered to at least 100, versus 2% without support.
WHY  Removing threats while directly managing the population worked best, as with Lord Howe Island’s woodhen, from 23 birds in the late 1970s to more than 1,600. The authors argue species should not be written off because their numbers look too small.

A Chilean seabird recovers 300-fold after island restoration
A census reported in Marine Ornithology counted 89,876 breeding pairs of Peruvian diving-petrel on Choros Island in November 2024, up from 300 pairs in 1980 and about 12,000 in 2013.
WHY  Habitat protection plus the 2013 removal of invasive rabbits by Chile’s forestry agency CONAF and Island Conservation let nesting ground expand. Next: recolonising nearby Chañaral Island with artificial burrows and recorded calls.

Essays & Arguments
Your newest note says pain only improves you if you make room to reflect on it. Today’s three pieces push on the next question: what lesson do you let it write? Cate Hall warns that hurt teaches lessons too strong and too general. Joyce’s boy turns one disappointing night into a verdict on himself. Sasha Chapin says some tensions never resolve, and expecting them to is its own trap.
 
Today’s question
What did your last setback teach you, and how would you know if the lesson is wrong?
ESSAY  Useful Fictions · Cate Hall
Until you get punched in the face ↗
Hall argues that what we learn in emotionally charged moments comes out overly strong and overly general, then quietly caps our agency. Her case: Ronda Rousey won 12 straight fights on one plan, lost to an opponent who solved it, and returned 13 months later with the same plan, losing in 48 seconds; she adds poker players who refuse to play pocket jacks properly and her own startup misreading one easy fundraise. The tension: reflection is how pain improves you, but reflection in the heat of it installs superstitions. Feeds your note on reflecting after pain.
ESSAY  Sasha’s Newsletter · Sasha Chapin
Without hope of resolution ↗
A gifted young friend asks Chapin how to balance ambition with acceptance; he tells her to give up on finding the balance point. Borrowing therapist Bruce Tift’s phrase, he argues useful philosophies contradict each other and people are cyclic, so you act on the situation in front of you and accept the swing. His catch: resolving to live without resolution is one more attempt at resolution. Feeds your notes on surrender and on going to bed early as rebellion.
STORY  UVA Open Anthology · James Joyce
Araby (1914) ↗
A boy on a dead-end Dublin street is infatuated with his friend’s older sister. When she mentions a bazaar called Araby that she cannot attend, he promises to bring her something, and the errand swells into a private quest slowed by school, an aunt’s gossiping guest, and an uncle who forgets. It has lasted because it fits all of adolescent longing into a few pages and lets one evening of disappointment become a moment of seeing. Feeds your note on pain and reflection.

WORKFLOW  simonwillison.net
Hide your slop PR descriptions behind <details><summary> ↗
Most changes to Willison’s projects now arrive as pull requests written by Claude Code or Codex, and he finds their descriptions accurate but too long to want to read. His fix: a one-paragraph human-approved opener, maybe a screenshot, with the full agent write-up folded into a collapsible <details> block labeled with the model name, which GitHub Markdown renders. A small habit with a bigger point: the agent does the exhaustive work; you supply the judgment about what deserves a reader’s time.

AWS Emulator and the engineer’s job when agents write the code ↗
AWS released a free, offline copy of more than 200 of its services whose code was written and tested entirely by agents: they write tests that must pass against real AWS, then build the emulator until it passes them. Swami Sivasubramanian says his own agent rebuild of an old replication engine stalled for four nights until it had a spec and a test environment, then took about two hours.

New York judge rules Novig’s markets are sports bets ↗
Judge Colleen McMahon denied Novig’s request to stop New York applying gambling law to its sports contracts, finding they are not swaps under federal commodities law because, to the customer, they differ from a sportsbook bet only in who takes the other side. She also criticized the CFTC; Novig appealed the next day.
