The British ‘king’ who drew Rome across the Channel may have been a queen
Bournemouth University archaeologist Miles Russell argues that Verica, the Atrebates ruler whose exile and appeal to Emperor Claudius preceded Rome’s AD 43 invasion, was a woman.
WHY  Verica’s coins show a slender female rider where other British rulers stamped male warriors, and Iron Age British names ending in “-a” were overwhelmingly feminine. Oxford epigrapher Roger Tomlin counters that Cassius Dio wrote the name in a male form.

Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for $41 billion in cash
Sanofi will pay $175 a share, a premium of approximately 46% to Insmed’s unaffected Nov. 18 close, for two marketed lung drugs (Brinsupri and Arikayce) and a phase 3 inhaled treprostinil program. It is funding the deal with debt and cash on hand and expects it to add to earnings per share by the end of 2028.
WHY  Sanofi says Insmed broadens its respiratory business and rebuilds its mid- and early-stage pipeline; trade press ties the urgency to the eventual loss of exclusivity on its top seller, Dupixent.

Nvidia’s board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO
Days after accepting his resignation, the board voted unanimously to restore Huang as president, CEO and director; interim chief Colette Kress stays CFO. Nvidia shares rose 31.48% to $165.05.

STORY  macmillanusa.com · Jack London
The Law of Life ↗
A nearly blind old man sits in the snow beside a small pile of wood while his tribe breaks camp and leaves him, as is the custom when the old can no longer keep up. Each stick he feeds the fire is time; while it burns he reasons through what nature asks of every living thing and remembers watching wolves run down an old moose when he was a boy. Published in 1901 and still one of London’s most anthologized stories, it turns one idea, that the individual is an episode and only the species lasts, into a countdown you can feel. About 2,500 words: read it for what that idea is worth when the fire gets low.
ESSAY  quantamagazine.org · Ben Brubaker
Network Researchers Nail Down Theory of ‘Perfect Resilience’ ↗
Two Berlin researchers proved that exactly four small network shapes make “perfect resilience” impossible: routing that, using only local information, delivers every packet whenever any path survives. The key is graph minors. Deleting or merging pieces of a network never makes looping worse, so the Robertson–Seymour theorem guarantees a finite list of forbidden shapes; Matthias Bentert found it by repeatedly trying to prove the list complete and hitting a counterexample, until he didn’t. Where the shapes are absent, a maze-solver’s rule (keep turning right) is enough. The catch: the shapes are so simple that dense, well-connected networks are the likeliest to contain them, so adding links can put the guarantee out of reach.

Typst: write proofs without fighting LaTeX ↗
Typst is a typesetting language built to match LaTeX’s power while being far easier to write: math goes between dollar signs with almost no backslashes (sqrt(5), phi.alt), the preview recompiles as you type, and error messages are readable. It has 57.2k GitHub stars, active development, and a free browser editor, so there is nothing to install unless you want the command line (brew install typst). For real analysis, that means problem sets that look like the textbook and take minutes to revise. First move: open typst.app, start a blank document, and retype one proof from this week’s set, epsilons and all.

Kalshi survives New York’s bid for an emergency order ↗
A state judge denied the attorney general’s request for a temporary restraining order, so Kalshi keeps offering event contracts in New York while the real question, whether federal derivatives law overrides state gambling law, heads to a preliminary-injunction hearing in early January. The pressure point is age: both sides return Nov. 30 to discuss limits on 18-to-20-year-olds, since New York’s sports-betting minimum is 21.

Sierra raises $1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation ↗
Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor’s AI-agent company says it passed $400 million in annual recurring revenue about two and a half years after launch; Greenoaks and Lightspeed led the round. The model worth noticing is pricing: customers pay per outcome, such as a resolved complaint or a processed claim, not per chat or token. It landed while public AI stocks were swinging through a correction.
