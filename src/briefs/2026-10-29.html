Israel’s election leaves both blocs short of a majority
With about 90% of votes counted, Netanyahu’s bloc holds 53 of 120 Knesset seats, Gadi Eisenkot’s 48, and the two Arab-led parties a record 19. Final results are due November 4.
WHY  The vote was a referendum on Netanyahu’s wartime record: he wants a right-wing government, Eisenkot wants him out, and the Arab parties say they will help oust him. Next, President Herzog picks a nominee; with no government in about 100 days, Israel votes again.

Iran weighs a Qatari plan to end the war as tankers hit mines in Hormuz
Iran says it is discussing a plan with mediators to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Its Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire after striking mines on what Iran calls an unauthorized southern route.
WHY  Iran wants ships on routes it controls near its coast; the US has urged the southern lane, which Trump declared mine-free in August. Tehran is expected to answer the Qatari draft by week’s end.

A basic income pilot cut hunger in three Amazon communities
A University of Bath evaluation of Cool Earth’s two-year cash pilot for 211 Asháninka and Yanesha people in Peru found households unable to afford nutritious food fell from 73% to 26%, and labour migration from 43% to 18%.
WHY  Unconditional payments let families stay on their land instead of leaving for wage work, and most interviewees reported reforestation and less burning. The authors call the results preliminary because the pilot had no control group.

4 
Restoring one mitochondrial fat reversed age-like muscle wasting in mice
University of Copenhagen researchers report in Nature Aging that falling cardiolipin drives the fibre shift seen in ageing muscle; restoring it to about two-thirds of normal reversed wasting and prevented early deaths in mice.
WHY  The shift looks protective, muscle trading power for defence against oxidative stress, which is why antioxidants made the mice worse. Cardiolipin is also lower in older people’s muscle; the team now wants to test whether it can be raised there.

ESSAY  Dynomight · Dynomight
Scattered thoughts on how doing math feels these days ↗
A working mathematician says AI has made him about 50% more productive, and that humans still matter because models are weak at building good abstractions and clear exposition. Pushing the model for simpler proof strategies once tightened a bound from 9.7 to 7.1. The tension: he wrote it without AI as a time capsule, and admits his defence of exposition might just be humans guarding their relevance. Feeds your note on outsourcing less of your thinking.
ESSAY  Contraptions · Venkatesh Rao
Cognitive Mercantilism ↗
Rao argues AI anxiety repeats mercantilism’s mistake: treating a resource as scarce and hoardable when abundance can create new demand. His mechanism is the “industrious revolution”: tea, sugar and tobacco made pre-industrial Britons choose to work more, lifting working days from about 250 a year to 300, and he expects cheap intelligence to make people invest more thought, not less. The open question: is intelligence far more rivalrous than tea?
ESSAY  From the Dining Table · Hannah Moel
The Wisdom We Reserve for Others ↗
A short first-person piece on Solomon’s paradox: we reason more wisely about other people’s problems than our own, and the gap is distance, not missing wisdom. It leans on Igor Grossmann’s experiments, where people reasoned better about strangers’ relationship conflicts than their own. The hook: the wisest king in the story failed at his own life, not for lack of knowledge. Feeds your note on advice you can give but not take.

WORKFLOW  Anthropic Engineering
Harness design for long-running application development ↗
Agents on long tasks drift and grade their own work too kindly, so Anthropic split building from judging: a generator writes code while a separate, skeptical evaluator clicks through the running app and fails anything below written thresholds. A solo run of a retro game maker cost $9 and shipped a broken play mode; the full harness cost $200 and worked. The cheap lessons transfer to your setup today: a fresh reviewer session with a checklist, handoff notes when context gets long, and one commit per passing feature.

Utah files felony charges against Kalshi and its founders ↗
Utah brought 13 counts of violating its gambling ban, plus one of profiting from illegal activity, naming CEO Tarek Mansour and COO Luana Lopes Lara, after a federal judge ruled state law applies and the 10th Circuit declined to pause enforcement. Kalshi calls it “crazy overreach” and says only federal law governs event contracts; the CFTC has sued at least 10 states on that theory. With cases in about 20 states, analysts expect the Supreme Court to settle who regulates the industry.
