France says Iran’s Revolutionary Guards hacked its supermarket freezers
The French foreign ministry attributes an attack on refrigeration controllers at more than 700 supermarket sites on the night of 11–12 October to Shahid Kaveh, a cyber unit it links to the IRGC. It says the attack caused fires and food losses of tens of millions of euros.
WHY  Paris says the attack broke UN norms on state behaviour in cyberspace that Iran has endorsed; it summoned Iran’s ambassador and will propose EU sanctions, and prosecutors have sought arrest warrants for the unit’s leaders. Tehran calls the accusations baseless.

WHO certifies that Barbados has ended mother-to-child HIV transmission
No infant in Barbados acquired HIV from their mother from 2022 to 2024. Over that period, 98% of pregnant women were tested and 98% of those living with HIV received treatment.
WHY  Antenatal care is free for every pregnant woman regardless of nationality, testing is voluntary and confidential, and one hospital handles 93% of births, so almost no one falls through the gaps. Barbados is one of 26 countries recognised; keeping the status requires sustained funding and wider HIV–syphilis testing.

UN counts 252 million hectares of nature under active restoration
A UNEP progress report finds restoration under way across 149 countries, an area the size of Kazakhstan, supporting more than a million green jobs.
WHY  Land restoration is on track for governments’ 2021 pledges, but only 23% of degraded marine and coastal areas and 5% of freshwater ecosystems are being revived. The report says annual investment must rise from $64bn to $296bn by 2030.

ESSAY  Substack · Dave Colina
Building agency in the age of agents ↗
Colina, who teaches entrepreneurship at CU Boulder, argues that AI makes people less capable when it removes the struggle that builds skill. His evidence is his own classroom: after ChatGPT arrived, student writing improved sharply, yet many students could not defend their arguments when questioned aloud. It pushes your note on agents taking the mundane tasks that gave you a sense of control.
ESSAY  Experimental History · Adam Mastroianni
We need more goat sacrifices ↗
Mastroianni argues that most well-meant social programs fail when tested, and we keep launching them because good intentions create an illusion of control. His examples: a $3 billion school-improvement program with no measurable effect, and One Laptop Per Child showing zero gains across 531 Peruvian schools. The twist is that the classic 1975 “illusion of control” study may not show what it claims, and he still thinks the cure is ritual humility plus more testing. A counterweight to your note on the mental path to a desired outcome.
STORY  scalar.lehigh.edu · Rabindranath Tagore
The Postmaster ↗
A young clerk from Calcutta is posted to a remote Bengal village, where his only company is Ratan, an orphan girl who keeps house for him. Evening talk about faraway families turns into reading lessons, then into a bond neither of them has a word for. Written in 1891, it has lasted because Tagore builds a whole relationship out of small domestic gestures and leaves you to weigh what each person meant to the other. It sits close to your note on wanting to be known deeply by a few people.

Remote Claude Code: programming like it’s the early 2000s ↗
Harper Reed shows how to run Claude Code on your own computer and drive it from your phone. The stack is small: Tailscale for a private network, an SSH app like Blink, mosh so sessions survive dropped connections, and tmux so several agents keep working while you’re in class. One caution: his aliases skip permission prompts, a risk to weigh before copying.

Mercor raises $750M at a $20B valuation to grade AI agents for companies ↗
Mercor, which supplies human experts to train and evaluate models, has doubled its valuation since October 2025 and reported annualized revenue above $2B in June. The new pitch is a custom benchmark for each company that tests agents against that firm’s own standards, plus recreated enterprise apps for agents to practise in. The open question: does “can this agent be trusted with this job” become an industry of its own?
PREDICTION MARKETS  crypto.news
Kalshi could face CFTC charges over how it launched contracts ↗
Citing a POLITICO report, the piece says the CFTC privately warned Kalshi of possible enforcement over whether dozens of new contracts were properly self-certified before listing. Kalshi calls it a procedural error involving a handful, many never live. Self-certification is how every exchange ships new markets, so how this settles sets the pace for the whole industry.
