Trump calls the Iran war “effectively over” but keeps the blockade
After meeting Qatar’s prime minister at the White House on Monday, Trump said the war was effectively over militarily and that the US would hold the status quo: the naval blockade of Iranian ports and the new Task Force Iron Gate on Iran’s land and Caspian trade. Iranian officials dismissed the remarks, and some deny that talks are under way.
WHY  Washington says the pressure protects its allies and ties ending the war to curbing Iran’s nuclear program; Tehran is pressing conditions including sanctions relief, release of frozen funds, recognition of a Hormuz shipping route agreed with Oman, and an end to fighting on all fronts. Next: whether Qatar’s mediation yields a written deal.

FDA approves the first factor XIa blocker to prevent repeat strokes
The FDA on Monday approved Bayer’s asundexian, taken with antiplatelet drugs, for adults after a mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke or high-risk mini-stroke. In the 12,327-patient OCEANIC-STROKE trial, 6.2% on the drug had an ischemic stroke versus 8.4% on placebo over a median 14.2 months.
WHY  Preventing clots usually costs bleeding; here major bleeding was 1.9% versus 1.7%, a difference that was not significant. Watch the label: the FDA flags higher bleeding in patients over 75 and in the first 90 days.

A brain-activated cell therapy cleared a fatal childhood tumor in mice
UCSF researchers engineered CAR-T cells, called B-SYNC, that switch on only after sensing a protein found throughout the brain and then attack diffuse midline glioma cells. In two mouse models with human tumors, the cancer was eliminated in every mouse; conventional CAR-T cells did not control it.
WHY  Diffuse midline glioma grows into the brainstem and is almost always fatal within a year, and the brain-only switch keeps the cells inactive elsewhere in the body. UCSF plans a human trial as early as April 2027.

I Miss Being Curious About Things That Have Nothing to Do With My Future ↗
Adult curiosity, she argues, has quietly become something that must justify itself as a project, a post, or a career move. CLASSIC  MIT Classics · Epicurus (tr. Hicks)
Letter to Menoeceus ↗
A roughly 1,900-word letter on what a good life actually requires, from the philosopher whose name became shorthand for indulgence, though he argues close to the opposite. He sorts desires into natural and necessary, natural but unnecessary, and empty, and defines pleasure as freedom from bodily pain and mental disturbance; simple living, he says, makes you independent of fortune, and death “is nothing to us.” It has lasted because it turns the question of happiness into an inventory you can run on your own wants. A sharp test for your note on being known well by a few rather than widely.

Locating the source of a mysterious beep with Claude ↗
A coding agent can solve a physical-world problem once you give it a way to sense the room. From his phone, Simon had Claude Code write a problem.md, dig up a 2007 acoustic-ranging paper, then build a Python server that turns five phones, tablets and laptops into browser “listeners” that detect beeps and triangulate them, with new code pushed to the devices over WebSocket so the agent could keep iterating. It found the beep within about 40 cm: an old carbon monoxide detector. The move worth copying for today’s hack day is the first one: write the problem down and let the agent research before it builds.
