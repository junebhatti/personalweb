FDA approves the first plasmid DNA immunotherapy, for a rare HPV-driven airway disease
Inovio’s Voceryq treats recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, where HPV causes airway growths that need repeated surgery. In a 32-patient trial, 7 of 26 people with three or more prior-year surgeries needed none afterward.
WHY  Two DNA plasmids, for HPV proteins and the immune signal IL-12, train the body to attack infected cells instead of cutting growths out. Accelerated approval means confirmatory trials come next.

A personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine cut recurrence or death by 42% in a phase 3 trial
Merck and Moderna report intismeran plus Keytruda cut the risk of recurrence or death at two years by 42% versus Keytruda alone in high-risk patients after surgery.
WHY  Each vaccine encodes up to 34 neoantigens from the patient’s own tumor, training T cells that Keytruda keeps switched on. The results are company-reported; peer review and an FDA decision come next.

Essays & Arguments
Today’s two pieces circle one failure: not saying the thing. Sarah Constantin argues that people stuck before a big change are usually dithering, and that everyone around them can already see what they want. Paul Graham’s disagreement hierarchy is the other half: when you do speak, aim at the central point, not the person or the tone. Both treat honesty as a skill you practice, not a mood you wait for.
 
Today’s question
Is most resentment just a disagreement you never said out loud?
ESSAY  Rough Diamonds · Sarah Constantin
Don’t Dither ↗
Constantin, a math PhD, says that when someone keeps describing a change they want and names no real obstacle, they should make it. Outsiders can’t predict the outcome, but they can hear the desire, the one thing the person can’t see in themselves. A commenter adds the fix: name and bound the vague worries, then move. It complicates your surrender note: letting go may still take one deliberate leap.
ESSAY  paulgraham.com · Paul Graham
How to Disagree ↗
Graham ranks disagreement in seven levels, from name-calling and tone policing up to refuting the central point. The levels describe form, not correctness, but they cap persuasiveness, and most meanness lives at the bottom. His hook: climbing the ladder makes people less hostile, not just more accurate. It gives structure to your note that disagreement isn’t zero-sum.

WORKFLOW  Simon Willison
The October inflection point for vibe game development ↗
Willison argues models can now make browser games that are fun, so the bottleneck has moved from code to concepts. His loop is copyable: a one-line prompt naming the engine (three.js or Phaser), an explicit ask to make it fun, playtest, short concrete feedback, then a coding agent once the prototype works. No game took him more than about four hours. The open question: if execution is cheap, what makes your idea worth building?
