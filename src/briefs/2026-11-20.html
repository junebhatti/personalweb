Trump, Xi and Putin sign a peace declaration that leaves out Ukraine and Iran

A billion missing years of rock may be the mantle’s doing
Across North America, northern Europe and North China, young sediment sits directly on far older rock, a gap called the Great Unconformity that spans 200 million to more than a billion years. University of Wollongong researchers, publishing in Nature, trace it to slow circulation deep inside the Earth.
WHY  In their simulations, mantle convection lifted these continental cores between 1 billion and 540 million years ago, so erosion stripped them; later they sank, seas returned and new sediment sealed the scar. The authors do not claim it was the only cause.

TPG will buy a majority of the small-business payments and accounts-payable software company for $63.25 a share in cash, with payments firm Corpay taking a minority stake. The price is a 31% premium to BILL’s 60-day average before talks were reported; closing is expected in the first half of 2027.
WHY  TPG points to the overlap of fintech, AI and payments, and Corpay says BILL complements its roadmap; it is the pair’s second accounts-payable take-private after AvidXchange, a sign sponsors are consolidating de-rated SMB software.

Essays & Arguments
Three pieces today about the stories told after the fact. Saloni Dattani finds that science’s lucky accidents mostly weren’t, and that the luck was added in the retelling. Nabeel Qureshi reads Proust as arguing the reverse: a life only makes sense once memory rebuilds it into something whole. And Saki’s 1914 story gives the sharpest grasp of narrative in the room to a fifteen-year-old. Between them sits a useful question for anyone who makes essays: when you tell what happened, are you recovering it or building it?

ESSAY  Works in Progress · Saloni Dattani
Science isn’t a series of lucky accidents ↗
Most famous “accidental” discoveries were not accidents; the lucky-break versions survive because they make better stories. Dattani checks them against letters and lab notes: colleagues said Fleming’s window was rarely opened, and Pasteur’s stale-broth accident was likely a deliberate experiment by his assistant Émile Roux, whose notes, like Röntgen’s, were burned. The unsettling part: if secrecy, feuds and burned notebooks decide the record, the origin stories you know are just the ones that survived.
ESSAY  nabeelqu.co · Nabeel Qureshi
On Reading Proust’s In Search of Lost Time ↗
Qureshi, an early Palantir engineer, argues Proust wrote the greatest novel by making it the story of its own creation: a man chases status and possessive love until he sees they were the wrong things to want. The engine is involuntary memory, the madeleine in tea, where a taste returns a whole past that deliberate recall cannot, and only then does a life become material for art. His surprising claim is that Proust is anti-Buddhist: the present is defective, and meaning arrives later, in remembering. It is the theory behind your charter-bus note.
STORY  Standard Ebooks · Saki
The Open Window ↗
A nervous city man, sent to the country to rest his nerves, calls on a stranger and is kept company by her composed fifteen-year-old niece, who explains why a French window stays open every evening. In about 1,100 words, Saki builds a ghost story inside a drawing room, then turns it in a way still taught as a model of the short-story ending. It lasts because it shows how completely a confident storyteller can rearrange what a listener sees.

Datasette Lite: query a spreadsheet inside a browser tab ↗
Datasette Lite runs Simon Willison’s data-exploration app entirely in your browser, with no install and no server, and this week’s rewrite makes it nearly identical to the full version. Load a CSV and you get sortable, filterable tables, facet counts and a SQL box, a faster way to interrogate your NYC restaurant data or Depop sales than scrolling a sheet. The catch: the file must sit somewhere that allows cross-site loading, and a public GitHub Gist is the easy route. First move: paste a CSV into a new Gist, copy its Raw link, open lite.datasette.io, click “Load CSV by URL” and run one query.

Novig signs marketing deals with seven NHL teams ↗
Novig announced partnerships Wednesday with the Bruins, Sabres, Hurricanes, Stars, Devils, Islanders and Flyers, covering broadcast spots, digital board signage and on-ice branding depending on the market: more NHL teams than any other prediction market, against Kalshi’s Blackhawks and Polymarket’s Rangers. STARTUPS  sec.gov · Palo Alto Networks
Palo Alto Networks buys Applied Compute for $4.5 billion in stock ↗
The security company agreed to acquire Applied Compute, a platform for post-training and serving open-weight AI models, along with its research team, in an all-stock deal. The bet is that enterprises will run open models tuned on their own data inside their own clouds, and someone has to make that safe; CEO Yash Patil becomes Palo Alto’s co-chief AI officer. Closing is expected in Palo Alto’s fiscal third quarter of 2027, pending regulators.
