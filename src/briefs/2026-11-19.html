One Pacific seabed rock held 245 tiny animals
Researchers from MARUM and NIOZ dissected 244 metal-rich nodules from the Clarion-Clipperton Zone and counted 4,139 animals living in their cracks, 17 per nodule on average, in communities unlike those in the surrounding mud (Scientific Reports).
WHY  The fist-sized nodules grow a few millimeters per million years and are rare hard shelter on a soft seafloor; their nickel, cobalt and copper make them a deep-sea mining target, and removing them would erase this habitat for good.

Today’s question
Is a flawless thing you knew you could make worth less than a flawed thing you weren’t sure you could?
POEM  RPO · Robert Browning
Andrea del Sarto ↗
A Florentine painter known as “the faultless painter” sits at dusk with his wife and talks himself through why his technically perfect work still ranks below Raphael’s flawed work. It is a dramatic monologue, the same form as “Ulysses”, so you hear a gifted man build his excuses in real time: money, marriage, fate, the comfort of being underrated. The famous line about a man’s reach exceeding his grasp comes from here, and it hits harder spoken by someone who has stopped reaching. About ten minutes.
ESSAY  Quanta · Shalma Wegsman
How M.C. Escher’s Art Creates Bizarre Mathematical Worlds ↗
Escher had no formal math training, yet his prints became material for working mathematicians. After the Alhambra’s tiles he taught himself tessellation, then matched his intuitions to Pólya’s paper on the 17 wallpaper symmetry groups; a crystallographer later judged he understood color symmetry better than her field, and in 2003 Lenstra and de Smit showed Print Gallery is effectively a complex exponential of an image. The show is at MoMath in Manhattan through February 8. Escher wrote that his growing fluency made him lonelier, which meets your note on how hard experience is to share.

DESIGN  ui-skills.com · Emil Kowalski
emil-design-eng: a design engineer’s rules, installed in your agent ↗
Emil Kowalski, who built the Sonner toast library, packaged his UI-polish rules as a skill your coding agent follows: animate only transform and opacity, never pop in from scale(0), use ease-out for anything opening, keep UI motion under 300ms, and skip animation on actions you repeat all day. Ask it to review and it returns a Before / After / Why table, so you learn the rule instead of just getting a patch. It is the quickest way to make a vibe-coded site stop feeling generated. First move: run npx skills add https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills --skill emil-design-eng in your site’s repo, then ask Claude Code to review one page’s animations.
