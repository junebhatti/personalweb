I've been struggling a lot lately, frantically searching for ways to detach from the constant stimulation that surrounds me. I'm unwillingly picking up my phone every five minutes, even with no notifications waiting. I'll be working on my computer and type in instagram just to check nothing of substance. I've been tainted by social media and the digital age to crave content of any form, at all times of the day. Let's take another example. I deleted social media, and I take a walk in public. How quickly do you realize that you are still consuming content constantly. Billboards, ads, and even music constantly playing in every shop and corner around you. The world is trying to silence us by taking away the time we have to think. When your mind is always being occupied, you lack the space for reflection and processing. We weren't built for that.

The worst part is that I'm entirely aware, but lack the willpower and conviction to make sustaining change. Deleting apps has get arounds, deactivating accounts feels radical, what's next...? Meditation?

I've been trying to understand myself better recently, and where the impulse comes from. I'm by no means there yet, but I think I have an idea. Scrolling is easy, and it hinders the need to think. Now seems obvious, and it is, but it's hard to internalize. On the other hand, critically thinking is  hard. You have to form an opinion, digest difficult content, generate ideas and pattern recognitions from limited information. It can be undesirable, especially when you can sit down, and aimlessly listen to other people tell you how to think.

That's why I have a few problems with social media:

1) Short form content  
Yes, you might have formulated a brilliant feed that teaches you about your interests and has wholesome, anti-brainrot content. But if I asked you to explain the reel from five swipes ago, could you? I definitely couldn't. Our brains weren't designed to compare and contrast and to see into the lives of so many people. In just 2 minutes, I could expose myself to 10+ different creators, with idealized, crafted lives that I desire, telling me how to live my life. It's easy to romanticize life in a 15 second reel, and when that's all you consume, it creates unrealistic expectations. It fires dopamine unrelenting dopamine into your brain, and the mundane becomes unwelcome. I've genuinely struggled to sit for 10 minutes without checking my phone for some odd reason.  
2) Commercialization  
There's a lot of interesting and creative people, but most are unfortunately there to get paid. The content you consume is a product of how to get you addicted. David Foster Wallace talks about media being created these days as a form of attacking our natural desires. 1) Meditation, finding the root of impulse  
I heard someone say that everything you want is just an excuse for an emotion you're trying to feel, and that's really stuck with me. Social media is so addictive because it lets us live an idealized version of life. I find myself watching a lot of traveling content, people in their twenties backpacking or taking roadtrips, something that I've been craving. I even consume content of people being creative, and I think it's a perfect show of the things that I want to do. How ironic it is that I waste hours watching other people live the life I want, awfully cuck-like.
